LEVEL 1 OF 75 MIN

The Frame

Every game needs a beginning, a pause and an end.

KEY TERMGame states
SCREEN FLOW

Wrap your gameplay in a frame.

  • Title screenSets the mood and lets the player get ready before anything can hurt them.
  • PauseReal life interrupts. Freeze everything, including timers and spawns.
  • Game overLosing needs a moment and a result, not a frozen screen.
  • One-button restartThe faster they can try again, the more they keep playing.
WHY IT WORKS

The game always knows which state it is in, so a clear start and end turns a toy into a session.

TRY THIS PROMPT

Add game states: a title screen that waits for Space or a tap, a pause menu on Esc that freezes everything, and a game over screen that shows the score and restarts with one button. Keep the gameplay exactly as it is.

LEVEL 2 OF 79 MIN

Juice

Feedback you can see and hear.

KEY TERMGame feel
POWER-UPS

Same rules. Way more satisfying.

  • Screen shakeA short, small shake makes hits and explosions feel heavy.
  • Hit pauseFreeze for a few frames on impact so the hit lands.
  • ParticlesSparks, dust and debris confirm what just happened.
  • Hit flashWhatever gets hit blinks white for a moment.
  • Squash & stretchThings stretch when they jump and squash when they land.
  • A sound for everythingJump, hit, pickup, hurt. Sound is half of game feel.
WHY IT WORKS

Nothing about the rules changes, but every action now answers back, so the player feels it. Too much hides what is happening, so add one effect at a time.

TRY THIS PROMPT

When the player hits an enemy, add a short hit pause, a small screen shake, a white flash on the enemy and a burst of particles. Put the strength of each effect in one settings object so I can tune them.

LEVEL 3 OF 76 MIN

Fair Enemies

Losing should feel like your fault, not the game's.

KEY TERMTelegraphing
ENEMY AI

Give every enemy a readable brain.

  • State machineOne state at a time: patrol, chase, wind-up, attack, recover.
  • PatternsPatrol, chase, flee. Players can only beat what they can learn.
  • TelegraphA wind-up before every attack: a flash, a sound or a pose.
  • Recovery windowAfter attacking, a short moment where it can be hit back.
WHY IT WORKS

Players accept losing when they could see it coming. A warning turns a cheap hit into a lesson.

TRY THIS PROMPT

Give the enemy a state machine: patrol, chase when the player is close, then attack. Before each attack add a half-second wind-up where it flashes and plays a sound, and after it a short pause where it can be hit.

REFERENCES
  • Game Programming Patterns: Stategameprogrammingpatterns.com
  • Game Maker's Toolkityoutube.com/@GMTK

LEVEL 4 OF 77 MIN

Progress

A reason to keep going, and a reason to come back.

KEY TERMDifficulty curve
EXPERIENCE POINTS

Let players feel themselves getting better.

  • Teach without textIntroduce each mechanic somewhere safe before it really matters.
  • Ramp, then breatheRaise the challenge in waves with calm moments between them.
  • Score and rewardsReward skill, not just time. Numbers going up feel good.
  • Save the high scoreA best score that survives a reload is a reason to come back.
WHY IT WORKS

Too easy gets boring, too hard gets frustrating. Waves keep players in the zone between the two, and the calm moments let them notice they are improving.

TRY THIS PROMPT

Make the game harder in waves: every 30 seconds raise enemy speed a little, with a short calm moment between waves. Show the score on screen and save the high score with localStorage so it is still there after a reload.

REFERENCES
  • MDN: localStoragedeveloper.mozilla.org
  • Game Maker's Toolkityoutube.com/@GMTK

LEVEL 5 OF 76 MIN

Boss Fight

When you add all of this, something will break. Here is how to win.

KEY TERMDebugging
BOSS STRATEGY

The AI can only fix what it can see.

  • 1. Describe the bugWhat you did, what you expected, and what happened instead.
  • 2. Bring evidenceOpen the console (F12) and paste the red errors. Add a screenshot.
  • 3. Shrink the problemOne change at a time. Find the last version that worked.
  • 4. Know when to retreatTwo or three failed fixes? Go back to your save and ask differently.
WHY IT WORKS

Asking for a diagnosis first stops the AI from guessing and piling new bugs on top of the old one.
